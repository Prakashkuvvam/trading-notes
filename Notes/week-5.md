This week marks the beginning of Phase 2 — Technical Analysis. Everything you learned in Month 1 was about understanding the market infrastructure. Now you will learn to read what the market is actually **doing** — through price.

# CHAPTER 1: WHY CANDLESTICKS AND NOT OTHER CHART TYPES

## Types of Charts

There are three main types of price charts:

### 1. Line Chart

A simple line connecting the **closing prices** of each period.

```
Price
  |         *
  |        / \
  |   *   /   \    *
  |  / \ /     \  / \
  | /   *       \/   \
  |/                  *
  +--------------------→ Time
```

**Advantage:** Clean and simple.
**Disadvantage:** Shows only closing prices. You lose all information about the open, high, and low of each period. You have no idea what happened **during** the period — only where it ended.

### 2. Bar Chart (OHLC Chart)

Each bar shows Open, High, Low, and Close.

```
    |           ← High
    |
  ──|           ← Open (left tick)
    |
    |──         ← Close (right tick)
    |
    |           ← Low
```

**Advantage:** Shows all four data points.
**Disadvantage:** Harder to read at a glance. You have to look carefully at which tick is open and which is close.

### 3. Candlestick Chart

Each **candle** shows Open, High, Low, and Close — but in a visually intuitive way.

**Advantage:** Immediately shows whether the period was bullish or bearish, how much the price moved, and where the key rejections happened.
**Disadvantage:** None significant. This is why virtually all traders use candlestick charts.

**We will use candlestick charts exclusively.**

# CHAPTER 2: ANATOMY OF A SINGLE CANDLESTICK

## The Four Data Points

Every candlestick represents a **specific time period**. On a 15-minute chart, each candle represents 15 minutes. On a daily chart, each candle represents one trading day.

Each candle has four pieces of information:

| Data Point | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| **Open** | The price at the **beginning** of the time period |
| **High** | The **highest** price reached during the time period |
| **Low** | The **lowest** price reached during the time period |
| **Close** | The price at the **end** of the time period |

---

## Parts of a Candlestick

A candlestick has three parts:

### 1. The Body

The body is the **thick rectangular portion** between the open and close.

- If the close is **above** the open → **Bullish candle** (typically green or white)
- If the close is **below** the open → **Bearish candle** (typically red or black)

### 2. The Upper Wick (Upper Shadow)

The thin line extending **above** the body. It represents the highest price reached during the period.

- Upper wick of a bullish candle: extends from the close to the high
- Upper wick of a bearish candle: extends from the open to the high

### 3. The Lower Wick (Lower Shadow)

The thin line extending **below** the body. It represents the lowest price reached during the period.

- Lower wick of a bullish candle: extends from the open to the low
- Lower wick of a bearish candle: extends from the close to the low

---

## Visual Representation

### Bullish Candle (Green/White)

```
        |           ← Upper wick (High)
        |
    ┌───────┐       ← Close (top of body)
    │       │
    │ BODY  │       ← Body (green/white)
    │       │
    └───────┘       ← Open (bottom of body)
        |
        |           ← Lower wick (Low)
```

**What happened:** Price opened at the bottom of the body, went down to the low (lower wick), went up to the high (upper wick), and closed near the top of the body. Buyers won this period.

### Bearish Candle (Red/Black)

```
        |           ← Upper wick (High)
        |
    ┌───────┐       ← Open (top of body)
    │       │
    │ BODY  │       ← Body (red/black)
    │       │
    └───────┘       ← Close (bottom of body)
        |
        |           ← Lower wick (Low)
```

**What happened:** Price opened at the top of the body, went up to the high (upper wick), went down to the low (lower wick), and closed near the bottom of the body. Sellers won this period.

---

## The Story Behind Every Candle

This is the most important concept of this entire week. **Every candle tells a story about the battle between buyers and sellers.**

### Example 1: Large Bullish Candle with No Wicks

```
    ┌───────┐
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    └───────┘
```

**Story:** Price opened at the low, buyers dominated the entire period, and price closed at the high. There was no significant selling pressure at any point. **Strong bullish conviction.**

### Example 2: Large Bearish Candle with No Wicks

```
    ┌───────┐
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    └───────┘
```

**Story:** Price opened at the high, sellers dominated the entire period, and price closed at the low. No significant buying pressure. **Strong bearish conviction.**

### Example 3: Small Body with Long Upper Wick

```
        |
        |
        |
        |
    ┌───┐
    └───┘
        |
```

**Story:** Price went up significantly during the period (long upper wick) but sellers pushed it back down. The final close was near the open. **Sellers rejected higher prices.** This suggests selling pressure.

### Example 4: Small Body with Long Lower Wick

```
        |
    ┌───┐
    └───┘
        |
        |
        |
        |
```

**Story:** Price went down significantly during the period (long lower wick) but buyers pushed it back up. The final close was near the open. **Buyers rejected lower prices.** This suggests buying pressure.

### Example 5: Long Wicks on Both Sides, Small Body

```
        |
        |
    ┌───┐
    └───┘
        |
        |
```

**Story:** Price went both up and down significantly, but closed near where it opened. Neither buyers nor sellers could maintain control. **Indecision.** The market is uncertain.

---

## Body Size Matters

| Body Size | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Large body** | Strong conviction. One side (buyers or sellers) dominated. |
| **Medium body** | Moderate conviction. |
| **Small body** | Weak conviction. Indecision. Neither side could dominate. |
| **No body (open = close)** | Complete indecision. This is called a **Doji**. |

## Wick Length Matters

| Wick | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Long upper wick** | Price went up but was **rejected**. Selling pressure exists at higher levels. |
| **Long lower wick** | Price went down but was **rejected**. Buying pressure exists at lower levels. |
| **No upper wick** | Buyers maintained control all the way to the close (bullish) or sellers maintained control from the open (bearish). |
| **No lower wick** | Sellers maintained control all the way to the close (bearish) or buyers maintained control from the open (bullish). |

# CHAPTER 3: TIMEFRAMES

## What Is a Timeframe?

The timeframe determines how much time each candlestick represents.

| Timeframe | Each Candle Represents | Used For |
|---|---|---|
| **1 minute** | 1 minute of trading | Scalping (not recommended for beginners) |
| **5 minutes** | 5 minutes of trading | Intraday entry and exit timing |
| **15 minutes** | 15 minutes of trading | Intraday trend identification |
| **1 hour** | 1 hour of trading | Intraday and short-term swing analysis |
| **4 hours** | 4 hours of trading | Swing trading |
| **Daily** | 1 full trading day | Swing trading and position analysis |
| **Weekly** | 1 full week (Monday to Friday) | Long-term trend analysis |
| **Monthly** | 1 full month | Very long-term analysis |

---

## Which Timeframes Will You Use?

For NIFTY intraday options trading:

| Timeframe | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Determine the overall trend and key support/resistance levels |
| **15-minute** | Identify intraday trend and market structure |
| **5-minute** | Time your entries and exits precisely |

**Do not use the 1-minute chart.** It's too noisy and will generate false signals that cost you money.

---

## How the Same Price Action Looks Different on Different Timeframes

Imagine NIFTY moves like this during one day (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM):

- Opens at 24,500
- Drops to 24,450 by 10:00 AM
- Rallies to 24,580 by 12:00 PM
- Drops to 24,520 by 2:00 PM
- Rallies to 24,560 by 3:30 PM

**On the daily chart:** You see ONE candle. Open: 24,500. Low: 24,450. High: 24,580. Close: 24,560. It's a bullish candle with a lower wick (showing buying support below 24,500).

**On the 15-minute chart:** You see approximately 25 candles. Some are bullish, some are bearish. You can see the intraday swings, the trend changes, and the momentum shifts.

**On the 5-minute chart:** You see approximately 75 candles. Even more detail — every minor pullback and push is visible.

**Key insight:** Higher timeframes show the **big picture**. Lower timeframes show the **details within that big picture**.

A single daily bullish candle might contain multiple bearish moves on the 15-minute chart. This is normal — the daily trend was up, but there were pullbacks along the way.

# CHAPTER 4: INDIVIDUAL CANDLESTICK PATTERNS

Now we study specific candlestick formations. I am covering the **10 most important patterns**. These are the ones you'll actually see and use. I'm not going to list 50 patterns because most of them are irrelevant variations of these core patterns.

**Critical rule:** A candlestick pattern by itself is NOT a trading signal. It must be read in CONTEXT — where it appears on the chart matters more than the pattern itself. I'll explain context after we learn the patterns.

---

## Pattern 1: Marubozu

### Bullish Marubozu

```
    ┌───────┐
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    └───────┘
```

**Characteristics:**
- Large green body
- No upper wick (or extremely small)
- No lower wick (or extremely small)
- Open = Low, Close = High

**What it means:** Buyers controlled the entire session from open to close. There was no significant selling pressure at any point. Extreme bullish strength.

### Bearish Marubozu

```
    ┌───────┐
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    │       │
    └───────┘
```

**Characteristics:**
- Large red body
- No upper wick
- No lower wick
- Open = High, Close = Low

**What it means:** Sellers controlled the entire session. Extreme bearish strength.

**Real-world occurrence:** Marubozu candles are rare on higher timeframes (daily). They're more common on 5-minute and 15-minute charts. When you see one on a daily chart, it usually follows a major news event.

---

## Pattern 2: Doji

A Doji has virtually no body — the open and close are at the same price (or very close).

### Standard Doji

```
        |
        |
      ──+──
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Open ≈ Close (very small or no body)
- Wicks can be of any length

**What it means:** Neither buyers nor sellers could gain control. **Indecision.** The market is at a turning point — or about to consolidate.

### Long-Legged Doji

```
        |
        |
        |
      ──+──
        |
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Open ≈ Close
- Very long upper and lower wicks

**What it means:** Extreme indecision. Price moved significantly in both directions but ended where it started. High volatility but no conviction.

### Dragonfly Doji

```
      ──+──
        |
        |
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Open ≈ Close ≈ High
- Long lower wick
- No upper wick

**What it means:** Price dropped significantly but buyers pushed it all the way back to the open. **Potential bullish reversal signal** (especially at support).

### Gravestone Doji

```
        |
        |
        |
        |
      ──+──
```

**Characteristics:**
- Open ≈ Close ≈ Low
- Long upper wick
- No lower wick

**What it means:** Price rose significantly but sellers pushed it all the way back to the open. **Potential bearish reversal signal** (especially at resistance).

---

## Pattern 3: Hammer

```
        |
    ┌───┐
    │   │
    └───┘
        |
        |
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Small body (can be green or red, but green is slightly more bullish)
- Long lower wick (at least **2x the body length**)
- Little or no upper wick
- Appears after a **downtrend**

**What it means:** Price fell significantly during the period (long lower wick) but buyers aggressively pushed it back up (close near the high). This shows **buying pressure** and **rejection of lower prices**.

**Signal:** Potential **bullish reversal** when it appears at the bottom of a downtrend or at a support level.

**Important:** A hammer appearing in the middle of a range or during an uptrend has no significance.

---

## Pattern 4: Inverted Hammer

```
        |
        |
        |
        |
    ┌───┐
    │   │
    └───┘
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Small body at the bottom
- Long upper wick (at least 2x the body length)
- Little or no lower wick
- Appears after a **downtrend**

**What it means:** Buyers tried to push the price up (long upper wick) but sellers pushed it back down. However, the fact that buyers are attempting to push higher after a downtrend suggests **potential reversal**.

**Signal:** Potential **bullish reversal**, but **weaker than a hammer**. Needs confirmation from the next candle.

---

## Pattern 5: Hanging Man

```
        |
    ┌───┐
    │   │
    └───┘
        |
        |
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Identical shape to a hammer
- BUT appears after an **uptrend** (not a downtrend)
- Small body, long lower wick, little upper wick

**What it means:** During an uptrend, sellers temporarily pushed the price down significantly (long lower wick). Even though buyers recovered, the selling pressure is a **warning sign**.

**Signal:** Potential **bearish reversal** when it appears at the top of an uptrend or at a resistance level.

**Key lesson:** The **same candle shape** (small body, long lower wick) is a **hammer** at the bottom (bullish) and a **hanging man** at the top (bearish). **Context determines the meaning.**

---

## Pattern 6: Shooting Star

```
        |
        |
        |
        |
    ┌───┐
    │   │
    └───┘
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Identical shape to an inverted hammer
- BUT appears after an **uptrend**
- Small body at the bottom, long upper wick, little lower wick

**What it means:** During an uptrend, buyers pushed price to new highs (long upper wick) but sellers overwhelmed them and pushed price back down near the open. **Strong rejection of higher prices.**

**Signal:** Potential **bearish reversal**. Stronger signal than a hanging man.

---

## Summary of These Four Patterns

| Shape | After Downtrend | After Uptrend |
|---|---|---|
| Small body + long lower wick | **Hammer** (bullish reversal) | **Hanging Man** (bearish reversal warning) |
| Small body + long upper wick | **Inverted Hammer** (bullish reversal attempt) | **Shooting Star** (bearish reversal) |

**The shape alone doesn't tell you whether it's bullish or bearish. The CONTEXT (where it appears — after an uptrend or downtrend) determines the interpretation.**

---

## Pattern 7: Engulfing Patterns

### Bullish Engulfing

```
    Day 1       Day 2
    ┌───┐    ┌────────┐
    │RED│    │        │
    │   │    │ GREEN  │
    └───┘    │        │
             │        │
             └────────┘
```

**Characteristics:**
- Two-candle pattern
- Day 1: Bearish (red) candle
- Day 2: Bullish (green) candle whose body **completely engulfs** Day 1's body
- Day 2's open is below Day 1's close, and Day 2's close is above Day 1's open
- Appears after a **downtrend**

**What it means:** Sellers were in control (Day 1), but buyers overwhelmed them completely on Day 2, erasing all of Day 1's losses and pushing higher. **Strong bullish reversal signal.**

### Bearish Engulfing

```
    Day 1       Day 2
    ┌───┐    ┌────────┐
    │   │    │        │
    │GRN│    │  RED   │
    └───┘    │        │
             │        │
             └────────┘
```

**Characteristics:**
- Two-candle pattern
- Day 1: Bullish (green) candle
- Day 2: Bearish (red) candle whose body completely engulfs Day 1's body
- Appears after an **uptrend**

**What it means:** Buyers were in control (Day 1), but sellers overwhelmed them on Day 2. **Strong bearish reversal signal.**

### What Makes a Good Engulfing Pattern

| Good Engulfing | Weak Engulfing |
|---|---|
| Day 2 body is significantly larger than Day 1 | Day 2 body barely covers Day 1 |
| Appears at key support/resistance | Appears in the middle of nowhere |
| Day 2 has high volume | Day 2 has low volume |
| Day 2 closes near its high/low | Day 2 has long wicks |

---

## Pattern 8: Morning Star (Bullish Reversal)

```
    Day 1       Day 2       Day 3
    ┌───┐                ┌────────┐
    │   │                │        │
    │RED│                │ GREEN  │
    │   │                │        │
    │   │      ┌─┐       │        │
    └───┘      └─┘       └────────┘
```

**Characteristics:**
- Three-candle pattern
- Day 1: Large bearish candle (continuation of downtrend)
- Day 2: Small body candle (can be green or red) with a **gap down** from Day 1's close — shows indecision
- Day 3: Large bullish candle that closes **above the midpoint of Day 1's body**

**What it means:** Day 1 shows sellers in control. Day 2 shows the selling pressure exhausting (small body = indecision). Day 3 shows buyers taking over aggressively. **Bullish reversal.**

**Note on gaps in Indian intraday charts:** True gaps (price opens significantly above/below previous close) are common on daily charts but less common on intraday charts (5-min, 15-min) because trading is continuous. On intraday charts, the "gap" might be just a small separation between candles.

---

## Pattern 9: Evening Star (Bearish Reversal)

```
    Day 1                 Day 3
    ┌────────┐            ┌───┐
    │        │            │   │
    │ GREEN  │   ┌─┐      │RED│
    │        │   └─┘      │   │
    │        │            │   │
    └────────┘            └───┘
    Day 1       Day 2     Day 3
```

**Characteristics:**
- Three-candle pattern
- Day 1: Large bullish candle
- Day 2: Small body candle (indecision) with a gap up from Day 1
- Day 3: Large bearish candle that closes below the midpoint of Day 1's body

**What it means:** Mirror image of Morning Star. **Bearish reversal.**

---

## Pattern 10: Spinning Top

```
        |
        |
    ┌───┐
    │   │
    └───┘
        |
        |
```

**Characteristics:**
- Small body (can be green or red)
- Upper and lower wicks that are **longer than the body**
- Similar to a Doji but has a visible body

**What it means:** Indecision. Both buyers and sellers were active but neither could dominate. The market is at a potential turning point.

**Signal:** By itself, a spinning top is not bullish or bearish. It's a **warning** that the current trend may be losing momentum. Look at the next candle for confirmation.

# CHAPTER 5: THE MOST IMPORTANT LESSON — CONTEXT

**This is the single most important section of this entire week. Read it carefully.**

Every candlestick pattern I've shown you above can be:

- A strong signal
- A meaningless signal
- A false signal

**It depends entirely on WHERE the pattern appears.**

---

## Context Rule 1: Where on the Chart

| Where Pattern Appears | Significance |
|---|---|
| **At a strong support level** | Bullish patterns are significant |
| **At a strong resistance level** | Bearish patterns are significant |
| **In the middle of a range** | Most patterns are meaningless noise |
| **At a key psychological level (24,000, 25,000)** | Patterns are more significant |
| **At a previous day's high or low** | Patterns are more significant |

### Example

A hammer candlestick at a **strong support level** where NIFTY has bounced three times before is a **significant signal**.

The same hammer candlestick in the **middle of the day's range** with no support/resistance nearby is **meaningless**.

---

## Context Rule 2: The Trend Before the Pattern

| Trend Before Pattern | Pattern Type | Valid? |
|---|---|---|
| Clear downtrend | Bullish reversal pattern (hammer, bullish engulfing) | **Yes** — potential reversal |
| Clear uptrend | Bearish reversal pattern (shooting star, bearish engulfing) | **Yes** — potential reversal |
| Sideways/range-bound | Any reversal pattern | **Usually not significant** |
| Clear uptrend | Bullish continuation patterns | Not a reversal — might continue |

**A reversal pattern is only relevant if there is something to reverse.**

A "hammer" after three green candles is NOT a reversal signal — there's no downtrend to reverse.

---

## Context Rule 3: The Candle After the Pattern (Confirmation)

**Never act on a single candlestick pattern alone.** Wait for the **next candle** to confirm.

### Example: Hammer at Support

| Scenario | What Happens Next | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Hammer forms at support | Next candle is a strong bullish candle closing above hammer's high | **Confirmed** — consider bullish trade |
| Hammer forms at support | Next candle is a small bearish candle closing below hammer's body | **Not confirmed** — wait or avoid |
| Hammer forms at support | Next candle is a big bearish candle breaking below support | **Failed** — the support broke. No bullish trade. |

---

## Context Rule 4: Volume

| Volume on Pattern Candle | Significance |
|---|---|
| **High volume** | Pattern is more significant — more participants are involved |
| **Low volume** | Pattern is less reliable — few participants, may not represent true sentiment |

A bullish engulfing pattern on high volume is much more convincing than one on low volume.

---

## Putting It All Together: A Complete Example

**Scenario:** NIFTY is at 24,500. There is a well-known support level at 24,300 (tested twice before in the last month).

NIFTY starts falling:
- Monday: Large red candle (24,500 → 24,420)
- Tuesday: Medium red candle (24,420 → 24,340)
- Wednesday: NIFTY opens at 24,320, drops to 24,290 (below support!), but then buyers push it back up and it closes at 24,350. This candle is a **hammer** with a long lower wick.

**Analysis:**
1. There IS a downtrend (three days of lower closes) ✓
2. The pattern appeared at a **key support level** (24,300) ✓
3. The lower wick shows **rejection** of prices below 24,300 ✓
4. Need to wait for **confirmation** on Thursday

**Thursday:**
- If NIFTY opens above 24,350 and rallies → **Confirmed bullish reversal**
- If NIFTY opens below 24,300 and continues falling → **Support broken, hammer failed**

**This is how professional traders think.** They don't just see a hammer and buy. They:
1. Check the location (support/resistance)
2. Check the preceding trend
3. Wait for confirmation
4. Then decide

# CHAPTER 7: COMMON MISTAKES BEGINNERS MAKE WITH CANDLESTICKS

## Mistake 1: Trading Every Pattern

**Wrong approach:** "I see a hammer. Buy!"

**Right approach:** "I see a hammer. Where is it? Is it at support? What was the trend before? What's the volume? I'll wait for confirmation."

## Mistake 2: Memorizing Dozens of Patterns

Some books list 40+ candlestick patterns. Most of them are minor variations of the patterns we've covered.

**The 10 patterns in this chapter cover 90%+ of what you need.** Master these 10 instead of superficially memorizing 40.

## Mistake 3: Looking Only at the Pattern, Not the Context

A bearish engulfing at resistance after a strong uptrend → High probability.

A bearish engulfing in the middle of a range with no clear trend → Almost meaningless.

## Mistake 4: Using Candlestick Patterns on Very Short Timeframes

On a **1-minute chart**, candlestick patterns are almost useless because:
- There's too much noise
- The patterns form and fail within seconds
- There isn't enough time for meaningful buyer-seller battles

**Use candlestick patterns on the 15-minute chart and above.** The 5-minute chart is acceptable but less reliable.

## Mistake 5: Ignoring the Bigger Picture

Before analyzing a 5-minute chart candle, always check:
- What is the daily trend?
- Where is the 15-minute trend?
- Are there key levels nearby?

A bullish hammer on the 5-minute chart means nothing if the daily and 15-minute charts are strongly bearish. The bigger trend dominates.

| **Gravestone Doji** | No body, long upper wick only | After uptrend at resistance | Bearish reversal |
| **Hammer** | Small body at top, long lower wick | After downtrend at support | Bullish reversal |
| **Inverted Hammer** | Small body at bottom, long upper wick | After downtrend at support | Weak bullish reversal |
| **Hanging Man** | Small body at top, long lower wick | After uptrend at resistance | Bearish reversal warning |
| **Shooting Star** | Small body at bottom, long upper wick | After uptrend at resistance | Bearish reversal |
| **Spinning Top** | Small body, wicks on both sides | At any trend extreme | Indecision, momentum weakening |

## Two-Candle Patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Location | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bullish Engulfing** | Small red + large green (engulfs) | After downtrend at support | Strong bullish reversal |
| **Bearish Engulfing** | Small green + large red (engulfs) | After uptrend at resistance | Strong bearish reversal |

## Three-Candle Patterns

| Pattern | Shape | Location | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning Star** | Large red + small body + large green | After downtrend at support | Strong bullish reversal |
| **Evening Star** | Large green + small body + large red | After uptrend at resistance | Strong bearish reversal |

# CHAPTER 9: HOW CANDLESTICKS CONNECT TO OPTIONS TRADING

You might be wondering: "I'm learning to trade NIFTY options. Why am I studying candlestick charts?"

Here's the connection:

### Step 1: You See a Pattern on the NIFTY Chart

You're looking at the NIFTY 50 chart (15-minute timeframe). NIFTY is at 24,500. You see a bullish engulfing pattern at the 24,450 support level.

### Step 2: You Form a Directional View

Based on the pattern + context, you expect NIFTY to move up from 24,450 toward 24,550 or higher.

### Step 3: You Choose an Option

Since you expect NIFTY to go up, you decide to buy a **NIFTY 24,500 CE** (or 24,450 CE for more delta).

### Step 4: You Define Entry, Stop-Loss, and Target

- Entry: Buy when NIFTY crosses above the engulfing candle's high
- Stop-loss: Below the engulfing candle's low (if NIFTY drops below this, the pattern failed)
- Target: Next resistance level (say 24,600)

### Step 5: You Execute and Manage

- If NIFTY moves up → Your call option premium increases → You sell for profit
- If NIFTY drops below your stop-loss → Your call option premium decreases → You sell for a limited loss

**The chart tells you WHEN and WHERE to trade. The option is the INSTRUMENT you use to trade.**

Without chart reading skills, you're essentially guessing the direction. With chart reading skills, you have a structured, evidence-based reason for your trade — even though the outcome is still uncertain.

### Answers

1. Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC).

2. If the close is above the open, the candle is bullish (green/white). If the close is below the open, the candle is bearish (red/black).

3. A long upper wick means price went up significantly during the period but was pushed back down by sellers. It shows rejection of higher prices and selling pressure at that level.

4. A long lower wick means price went down significantly during the period but was pushed back up by buyers. It shows rejection of lower prices and buying pressure at that level.

5. A Marubozu is a candle with a large body and virtually no wicks (open = low and close = high for bullish, or open = high and close = low for bearish). A bullish Marubozu indicates extreme bullish strength — buyers controlled the entire session with no significant opposition.

6. A Doji is a candle where the open and close are approximately equal (very small or no body), with wicks on one or both sides. It indicates indecision — neither buyers nor sellers could gain control. It often appears at potential turning points.

7. A Dragonfly Doji has the open/close/high at the top with a long lower wick only — potential bullish reversal signal. A Gravestone Doji has the open/close/low at the bottom with a long upper wick only — potential bearish reversal signal.

8. A Hammer has a small body near the top of the candle with a long lower wick (at least 2x the body) and little or no upper wick. It must appear after a downtrend, ideally at a support level, to be a valid bullish reversal signal.

11. A Bullish Engulfing is a two-candle pattern where a small bearish candle is followed by a large bullish candle whose body completely engulfs the previous candle's body. It appears after a downtrend at support and signals a strong bullish reversal.

12. A Morning Star is a three-candle pattern: a large bearish candle, followed by a small-bodied candle (indecision), followed by a large bullish candle that closes above the midpoint of the first candle's body. It signals a bullish reversal.
